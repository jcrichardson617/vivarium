# Reptilytics email outreach kit

Goal: get relevant websites to link to Reptilytics. Each link from a herp society, vet clinic or
care site tells Google we're trustworthy, and sends real keepers our way.

## How to do it

- **5–10 emails a week**, each personalised (use their name, mention something on their site).
  Mass emails get ignored or marked as spam.
- Send from **Admin → Email** on reptilytics.com (goes out from info@reptilytics.com; replies
  land in your Gmail). Sign as "The Reptilytics Team".
- **Point each audience at the page that helps *them*** (see the table) — not the home page.
- No attachments. One link per email is best, two at most.
- Track who you emailed and when in a simple spreadsheet. **Follow up once** after ~10 days, then stop.
- **Breeder replies are product research:** log their answers (software used, likes, frustrations)
  in the spreadsheet too. Patterns across 10–20 replies tell us what to build next.

| Audience | Link them to | Why they'd care |
|---|---|---|
| Herp societies | Home page (record keeping), then /tools | A free place for members to log their animals, plus free tools; the society's links page |
| Reptile vets | /tools/prey-size, /tools/enclosure-size | Handouts for clients; fewer husbandry-related visits |
| Care-sheet & resource sites | /genetics, species morph pages, /research | Cite-able data and tools for their readers |
| Breeders | /genetics, morph pages for their species | Explain pairings to buyers; and we ask what software they use and what they'd change |

## Who to contact

**Herp societies (start local, then regional)**
- Directory of US societies by state: https://www.kingsnake.com/orgs/index.php
- Society for the Study of Amphibians and Reptiles (SSAR) — large, academic; good for the research pages
- Regional examples: Chicago, Bay Area, Tucson, DFW, Texas, New England, Virginia, Colorado,
  Gainesville, Southern Nevada herp societies. Most have a "Links" or "Resources" page — that's
  where you want to be listed.

**Reptile & amphibian vets**
- ARAV "Find A Vet" directory: https://arav.org (also mirrored at https://kingsnake.com/vets.html)
- Look for clinics whose websites have a "Client resources" or "Care sheets" page.

**Care & resource sites**
- Sites with "useful links" / resource pages, e.g. https://www.nrz.ie/?p=8467 (Irish reptile FAQ
  with grouped links), https://thebiodude.com (care sheet lists), https://www.morereptiles.com
- Small bloggers and care-sheet writers are more likely to reply than big brands.

**Breeders**
- Breeders with their own websites (not just marketplace listings), especially those with a
  "Genetics" or "FAQ" page for buyers.

Hi Jon and Tracy,

We'd like to introduce Reptilytics, a record-keeping site for keepers and breeders, and before we
go further we'd love to learn from people who actually run collections. Would you mind answering

1. What do you use to keep records today (iHerp, Husbandry.Pro, MorphMarket's Animal Manager,
   a spreadsheet, paper…)?
2. What do you like about it?
3. What frustrates you, or what do you wish it did?

Even one-line answers help a lot!

For context, here's what Reptilytics does with your records today, beyond just storing them:

- **A daily dashboard** that lists who's due or overdue to eat (most overdue first) and which
  clutches are close to hatching.
- **Feeding trends:** a 30-day chart of meals fed versus refused, and your acceptance rate for
  the week across the whole collection.
- **A watch list** that flags any animal that refuses two meals in a row or drops more than 10%
  of its body weight, plus animals that haven't been weighed in 30+ days.
- **Weight sparklines** next to every animal in your collection list, so a stalled or falling
  weight stands out at a glance.
- **Breeding records** from pairing through clutches, eggs and incubation to hatchlings, with
  hatch rates per clutch and family trees that build themselves as hatchlings inherit their
  parents' genetics.
- **New research for your species:** recently published papers on the animals you actually keep,
  right on your dashboard.

Next we're working on anonymous community comparisons, like what normal growth looks like for a
species, so if there's a number you've always wanted to see for your animals, tell us!

If you're up for trying Reptilytics itself, we'd happily give you free premium for life as an
early tester.
